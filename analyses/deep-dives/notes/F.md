### Business in one paragraph

Ford is a global automaker organized around Ford Blue for combustion and hybrid vehicles, Ford Model e for electric vehicles, Ford Pro for commercial vehicles and services, and Ford Credit. Profit is concentrated in trucks, SUVs, vans, parts, software-enabled fleet services and financing rather than pure EV volume. F-Series, Super Duty, Transit, Bronco and commercial uptime products are the franchise. The investment case hinges on whether Ford Pro and truck pricing can fund EV restructuring, warranty improvement, shareholder returns and the next product cycle without another quality or labor-cost setback.

### Bull case vs bear case

- **Bull:** Ford Pro compounds, Super Duty and F-Series pricing stays rational, warranty costs trend down, and hybrid demand buys time for a cheaper EV platform. Model e losses narrow without starving the profitable truck and commercial franchises.
- **Bear:** EV losses remain large, warranty issues keep recurring, tariffs and labor costs compress margins, and Ford Credit weakens in a downturn. A delayed affordable EV platform would leave Ford defending old profit pools while competitors reset price expectations.

### Catalysts to watch (next 6–12 months)

1. Late October 2026 Q3 results: warranty accruals, Ford Pro margin and tariff commentary.
2. Updates on Universal EV platform milestones and battery sourcing.
3. Super Duty, F-Series and hybrid order trends.
4. Ford Credit delinquencies and used-vehicle residual values.
5. Capital return guidance versus restructuring and EV investment needs.

### What would change the verdict

- **More constructive:** multiple quarters of lower warranty cost, stable truck pricing, Ford Pro growth, and clearer evidence that Model e losses have peaked.
- **More cautious:** renewed recall spikes, weaker commercial demand, credit deterioration, or another major EV delay/write-down.
