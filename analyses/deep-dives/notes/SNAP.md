### Business in one paragraph

Snap is a camera-first social platform built around Snapchat: messaging, Stories, Spotlight short video, Discover media, Snap Map, AR lenses and Bitmoji. Revenue is still overwhelmingly advertising, with demand tied to auction pricing, direct-response budgets and brand campaigns, while Snapchat+ subscriptions and AR tools add a smaller but strategically useful direct-revenue stream. The company competes for attention and ad budgets against Meta, TikTok, YouTube and retail-media networks, but it has a distinct youth audience and a strong AR developer ecosystem. The investment case hinges on whether Snap can turn durable user growth, especially outside North America, into materially better ARPU while funding AI and AR hardware without recurring dilution and operating losses.

### Bull case vs bear case

- **Bull:** Snap keeps growing daily users, improves lower-funnel ad performance and makes Snapchat+ a durable margin contributor. AR remains a brand differentiator without overwhelming the cost base, and operating leverage finally shows up as ad pricing recovers.
- **Bear:** Engagement shifts to larger video platforms, measurement remains weaker than peers and AR hardware consumes cash before it becomes a product. In that case, revenue growth may not translate into sustainable free cash flow, and dilution stays part of the story.

### Catalysts to watch (next 6–12 months)

1. Evidence that newer ad formats are improving conversion outcomes, not just impressions.
2. North America and Europe ARPU trends, especially versus international DAU growth.
3. Snapchat+ subscriber growth, churn and feature adoption.
4. Spending discipline in AI, cloud infrastructure and Spectacles development.
5. Any regulatory developments around minors, app stores or privacy-safe measurement.

### What would change the verdict

- **More constructive:** sustained ARPU acceleration in developed markets, positive free cash flow after SBC and evidence that AR investment is becoming a software platform rather than a hardware cash drain.
- **More cautious:** slowing engagement in core youth cohorts, renewed heavy losses, weaker advertiser ROI versus Meta or TikTok, or a regulatory change that materially limits targeting or youth monetization.
