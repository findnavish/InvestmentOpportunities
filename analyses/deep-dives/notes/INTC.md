### Business in one paragraph

Intel designs and manufactures CPUs, accelerators, networking chips and foundry services. The legacy profit engine is client and server x86 CPUs, but the strategic pivot is Intel Foundry: regaining process leadership, filling fabs with external customers, and using US and European manufacturing as a geopolitical advantage. The investment case hinges on whether Intel 18A and follow-on nodes can ship competitive products, whether foundry customers commit real volume, and whether cost cuts can stabilize cash flow before depreciation and fab spending overwhelm the turnaround.

### Bull case vs bear case

- **Bull:** 18A products ship well, Intel Foundry signs credible external volume, and cost discipline stabilizes free cash flow. A domestic leading-edge manufacturing option becomes strategically valuable, giving Intel time to repair CPU share and margins.
- **Bear:** Foundry customers wait for later nodes or stay with TSMC, internal products miss performance or yield targets, and fab depreciation rises faster than revenue. In that scenario government support delays but does not solve the economic problem.

### Catalysts to watch (next 6–12 months)

1. Late October 2026 Q3 results, especially foundry losses, client demand and server share commentary.
2. Panther Lake, Clearwater Forest and 18A yield or volume milestones.
3. External foundry customer announcements with real volume commitments.
4. Further restructuring, asset sales or capex deferrals.
5. Updates on CHIPS funding milestones and fab construction pacing.

### What would change the verdict

- **More constructive:** 18A yield evidence, improving gross margins, foundry backlog from external customers, and server share stabilization.
- **More cautious:** further product delays, foundry losses widening, negative cash-flow revisions, or customers shifting more workloads to Arm and accelerators.
