### Business in one paragraph

Micron is a memory manufacturer focused on DRAM, NAND and high-bandwidth memory used in PCs, phones, data centers, automotive and industrial devices. Memory is cyclical, but AI servers have shifted investor focus toward HBM, advanced DRAM and enterprise SSDs. Micron competes mainly with Samsung and SK hynix, and its returns depend on industry supply discipline, technology transitions and pricing. The investment case hinges on whether AI memory demand can keep DRAM tight enough to offset normal consumer-device cycles and the heavy capital spending required for leading-edge fabs.

### Bull case vs bear case

- **Bull:** HBM demand stays supply-constrained, Micron gains share at leading AI customers, and broader DRAM pricing remains disciplined. The cycle becomes less volatile as AI servers absorb a larger share of bits.
- **Bear:** Competitors add HBM and DRAM capacity faster than demand, consumer devices soften, or Micron misses qualification windows. The market could then revert to the familiar memory pattern of falling prices and margin compression.

### Catalysts to watch (next 6–12 months)

2. Customer qualifications for HBM4 and next-generation AI platforms.
3. DRAM and NAND pricing trends through PC, smartphone and server channels.
4. Capital-spending guidance and supply-discipline commentary from all major memory makers.
5. Idaho and New York fab construction milestones and CHIPS funding updates.

### What would change the verdict

- **More constructive:** HBM share gains, positive DRAM price revisions, and capex discipline across the industry.
- **More cautious:** signs of oversupply, customer qualification delays, NAND weakness spreading to DRAM, or lower AI server build expectations.
