### Business in one paragraph

Rivian makes electric adventure vehicles, commercial vans and vehicle software. Current revenue comes from R1T pickups, R1S SUVs, electric delivery vans for Amazon and other fleets, services, regulatory credits and, increasingly, technology collaboration with Volkswagen. The future case is about the lower-cost R2 and R3 families, a simpler bill of materials, higher plant utilization in Normal, Illinois, and eventually the Georgia site. Rivian has a respected brand and strong product reviews, but remains cash-burning. The investment case hinges on whether it can bridge to R2 launch without excessive dilution and then scale profitably in a crowded EV market.

### Bull case vs bear case

- **Bull:** R2 launches on time with lower build cost, VW funding and licensing reduce dilution, and Rivian turns gross profit into operating leverage. The brand then expands from premium adventure vehicles into a credible mass-market SUV franchise.
- **Bear:** R2 timing slips, launch costs exceed plan, EV demand softens, and cash burn remains high enough to require more equity before profitability. VW collaboration may help technology but cannot fix manufacturing economics if volumes disappoint.

### Catalysts to watch (next 6–12 months)

1. R2 validation, tooling progress and supplier readiness before launch.
2. Quarterly cash burn, liquidity and any additional capital actions.
3. Gross margin excluding regulatory credits and launch costs.
4. Volkswagen JV milestones, software integration and any third-party licensing interest.
5. Amazon and non-Amazon van orders as evidence of commercial-fleet diversification.

### What would change the verdict

- **More constructive:** R2 launches on schedule with strong reservations, gross margin stays positive through ramp preparation, and VW milestones arrive without major cash needs.
- **More cautious:** R2 delays, renewed negative gross margins, rising warranty issues, or financing that materially dilutes existing holders.
